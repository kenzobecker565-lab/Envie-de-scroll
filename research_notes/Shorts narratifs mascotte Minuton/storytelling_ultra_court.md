# Storytelling craft for ultra-short vertical videos (15-60 s) with a recurring character (mascot "Minuton", Scroll Up)

Note on evidence quality (read first): almost all "hard numbers" about hooks and retention in short-form come from vendor blogs (OpusClip, Quso, Metricool, Virlo, CapCut, Subscribr) that rarely publish their methods. Platform-published studies (TikTok Marketing Science, YouTube) are mostly cited second-hand. Classic craft sources (Pixar/Emma Coats, Kenn Adams, Chuck Jones) and academic sources (Cutting on shot length, McGraw & Warren on humor) are solid but not short-form-specific. Each finding below is tagged accordingly where it matters.

## 1. Hooks in the first 1-3 seconds: types and evidence of effectiveness

### Takeaway
There is broad practitioner consensus (but little rigorous public evidence) that the swipe/stay decision happens in roughly 1.5-3 s, and that the most effective openings are concrete and visual (a result, a reaction, motion within the first second, a captioned first line, an open question/promise). For a mascot, in-media-res and visual pattern interrupts are the natural fit.

### Cited Findings
- One YouTube Shorts guide claims 50-60% of Shorts drop-offs happen in the first 3 seconds (vendor claim, method not published) — [OpusClip: Ideal Shorts length & format](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention); Quso similarly argues most retention loss happens in the first three seconds — [Quso retention benchmarks](https://quso.ai/research/video-retention-benchmarks)
- A secondary source attributes to TikTok Marketing Science that the swipe-or-stay decision comes within ~1.7 s (no study name/date given) — [Everything PR: TikTok creative codebook](https://everything-pr.com/the-tiktok-creative-codebook-sound-on-native-first-90-10-rule); OpusClip says the "first decision" comes at 1.5 s and the opening is judged by 3 s (internal claim) — [OpusClip: 8 things viral TikToks do in the first 3 seconds (2026)](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-tiktoks-first-3-seconds-2026)
- Soundstripe attributes to TikTok for Business that 63% of the highest click-through videos hook viewers within the first three seconds (second-hand) — [Soundstripe: How to get more views on TikTok (2026)](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-to-get-more-views-on-tiktok)
- TikTok executives presented 40+ studies at the ARF (2022); WARC's summary recommends "intelligent usage of the first few seconds" and leveraging audio, music, text and storytelling (details paywalled) — [WARC, 7 Feb 2023](https://www.warc.com/content/article/event-reports/seven-ways-to-improve-the-effectiveness-of-tiktok-ads/149476)
- OpusClip's "8 moves" for the first 3 s (based on 34,635 viral TikToks scored by its own engine, unverified): (1) open on the most concrete frame — the result, a reaction, the finished product; (2) one emotional register, with music/face/captions/cut aligned; (3) intentional motion within 1 s; (4) front-load the most specific detail; (5) make a promise / open loop; (6) caption the first line within 0.5 s, large and high-contrast; (7) cut ~0.3 s before the natural end of a beat (mid-action, at peak tension); (8) clean audio within the first 0.5 s (voice peaks -6 to -3 dB, 200 ms fade-in on music) — [OpusClip 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-tiktoks-first-3-seconds-2026)
- Practitioner retention guides recommend opening in the middle of the action or with a curiosity-driven question overlaid on the first frames — [GoFaceless: best retention strategies for Shorts](https://www.gofaceless.ai/de/blog/best-retention-strategies-youtube-shorts); [Subscribr: perfect YouTube Short](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/perfect-youtube-short-length-structure-hooks)
- "Open loop" logic (withhold the answer, deliver it later) is framed via the Zeigarnik effect: unfinished narratives hold attention; nested loops = several stories opened then closed in reverse order — [Nested loops concept](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/nested-loops/); one creator guide argues that giving the answer in the first 5 s "kills" retention (opinion) — [Joyspace: open loop technique](https://joyspace.ai/open-loop-technique-force-viewers-watch-end)
- Unverified headline numbers to avoid quoting as fact: "strong hooks = ~60% higher retention" (Virlo, cites only "recent research") — [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/why-the-first-few-seconds-matter); "average TikTok watch time 3.75 s" (Metricool, no source) — [Metricool newsletter](https://newsletter.metricool.com/p/8-ideas-for-your-short-videos-and-a-100-meter-world-record)

### Inferences
- Hook typology usable for Minuton (synthesised from the sources above): (a) visual pattern interrupt — an unexpected image/motion in frame 1 (e.g., Minuton being sucked into an infinite feed); (b) in-media-res — open on the climax of trouble, then rewind; (c) curiosity gap / promise — on-screen text such as "Minuton a tenu 7 secondes sans scroller..." ; (d) question — "Et si ton pouce faisait grève ?"; (e) result-first — show the finished creative object, then how it happened.
- For a mascot account the first frame should already contain the character's face showing one clear emotion (rule 2 above), because the character's face is the brand asset and the emotional signal at once.
- Since several sources disagree on sound-off share (see section 5), the hook should work both silently (visual + caption) and with sound.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or platform-published primary study on first-3-second hook types was found; comparative effectiveness of question vs. in-media-res vs. visual interrupt is not established by any public controlled data.
- TikTok's original Marketing Science pages for the 1.7 s and 63% figures could not be located.

## 2. Micro narrative structures: setup/conflict/payoff, compressed story spine, perfect loops, twists, "part 2" cliffhangers, running gags

### Takeaway
The Story Spine (Kenn Adams, popularised via Pixar's 22 rules) compresses well into a 15-60 s beat sheet; Pixar's rule "come up with your ending first" is especially relevant for twist and loop endings. Since March 31, 2025, every replay of a YouTube Short counts as a view, so seamless loops have a direct metric payoff. Cliffhangers work when each episode still delivers a payoff.

### Cited Findings
- Story Spine (Pixar rule #4 per Emma Coats, 2011): "Once upon a time there was ___. Every day, ___. One day ___. Because of that, ___. Because of that, ___. Until finally ___." — [Laughing Squid: 22 rules](https://laughingsquid.com/22-rules-of-storytelling-by-pixar-storyboard-artist/)
- Kenn Adams says he invented the Story Spine (late 1980s, while working with a New York improv company and teaching a middle-school writing workshop); his version has 8 fixed starters: once upon a time / every day / but one day / because of that (x3) / until finally / ever since then. He says a student of his later taught it to Pixar's writers — [NPR, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5750619); a Substack explainer dates it to 1991 (date conflict) — [Improv Basics: Story Spine](https://sidrdesai.substack.com/p/improv-basics-story-spine)
- Other Pixar/Coats rules directly usable for micro-stories: #7 "Come up with your ending before you figure out your middle"; #5 "Simplify. Focus. Combine characters. Hop over detours"; #22 "What's the essence of your story? Most economical telling of it?"; #19 "Coincidences to get characters into trouble are great; coincidences to get them out of it are cheating"; #12 "Discount the 1st thing that comes to mind... Surprise yourself"; #16 "What are the stakes?" — [Laughing Squid](https://laughingsquid.com/22-rules-of-storytelling-by-pixar-storyboard-artist/)
- YouTube changed Shorts view counting effective March 31, 2025 (announced March 26): each play or replay counts as a view with no minimum watch time; the old metric survives as "engaged views" in Advanced Mode — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); [Brandwatch help](https://social-media-management-help.brandwatch.com/hc/en-us/articles/26214254055709-YouTube-Changes-to-Views-Counts-on-Shorts); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/youtube-shorts-will-see-more-view-counts-earnings/489190)
- A visible "outro" (saying goodbye, fade to black, end screen) signals the end and triggers a swipe; prefer an abrupt or integrated ending unless deliberately building a loop — [Subscribr: low Shorts watch time](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-low-watch-time-boost)
- Story loops vs. seamless loops are described as deliberate structures (timing, audio cuts, visual transitions) that drive replays — [Faceless.so: story loops and seamless loops](https://faceless.so/blog/looping-short-videos-tutorial-story-seamless-loops)
- Cliffhangers: a weak cliffhanger withholds everything ("wait for part 2" with no value); a good one adds to an already delivered payoff and creates a controlled gap between what the viewer knows and wants to know next — [CapCut: cliffhanger endings in short-form series](https://www.capcut.com/create/cliffhanger-endings-short-form-video-series); a creator plan recommends a few related series where each video delivers one clear payoff — [Stack Influence: 30-day Shorts plan](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/youtube-shorts-strategy-a-30-day-plan-for-creators)
- Running gags need strict rules: Chuck Jones's Road Runner rules (from *Chuck Amuck*) — the coyote is only ever defeated by his own incompetence or Acme failures, no dialogue except "Beep-beep!", always the desert, gravity is the main enemy, he is "more humiliated than harmed", and he "could stop at any time — if he were not a fanatic." Jones: without rules "comedy slops over the edges." Rules themselves generated recurring gag variations (painted tunnel/line) — [MeTV Toons: Chuck Jones' rules](https://metvtoons.com/lists/chuck-jones-rules-of-the-road-runner); [Wikipedia: Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner)
- Gag-driven formats work at very short durations: of Chikn Nuggit, BuzzFeed's Sievers says "the jokes only take about 10 seconds to get across" — [Tubefilter, May 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/05/11/creators-on-the-rise-the-land-of-boggs/amp/)
- Length: Quso reports 11-20 s Shorts earn the highest median views; another (vendor) analysis says <15 s Shorts earn 4.6x more views than 46-60 s (both unverified). YouTube raised the Shorts cap to 3 min in Oct 2024 — [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention); [Quso](https://quso.ai/research/video-retention-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- Compressed Story Spine for a 30 s Minuton episode (inferred timing, not sourced): 0-2 s "One day" (hook: the disruption shown first, in media res) → 2-5 s "Every day" (one-shot reminder of the normal: Minuton's thumb twitching toward the feed) → 5-20 s "Because of that" x2 (escalation; each beat must be causal, not "and then") → 20-27 s "Until finally" (payoff/twist: the urge becomes a tiny creative act) → 27-30 s "Ever since then" collapsed into a loop-back or a running-gag button.
- Three ending modes to rotate: (1) perfect loop — last frame/line flows into the first (e.g., ends on Minuton's thumb reaching for the phone, which is the opening frame); (2) twist — set up an expectation in the first 3 s and invert it (Pixar #12: discard the first 5 ideas); (3) cliffhanger — only after a mini-payoff, labelled "Partie 2" in the series.
- Write a "Minuton bible" of Road-Runner-style rules (e.g., Minuton is only ever defeated by his own urge to scroll, never by a villain; the feed is the "gravity"; the solution is always a small creative act; he is humiliated, never hurt). Rules make running gags generative and on-brand.
- Because replays count as views on YouTube since March 2025, loop endings are now a measurable lever, but "engaged views" should be the KPI to avoid self-deception.

### Gaps
- No public data comparing performance of loop vs. twist vs. cliffhanger endings for animated mascots.
- No official YouTube/TikTok guidance on "part 2" series mechanics found; evidence is practitioner-only.

## 3. Recurring-character arcs: making a mascot lovable; episodic vs. serialized

### Takeaway
Lovability comes from trying (not succeeding), clear opinions/flaws, a consistent persona with a running motivation, and a supporting cast with contrasting temperaments. Duolingo's Duo shows a consistent "pushy friend" persona works episodically, and that occasional serialized "events" (the 2025 death/resurrection arc) can create huge spikes when tied to an in-app action.

### Cited Findings
- Pixar/Coats: #1 "You admire a character for trying more than for their successes"; #13 "Give your characters opinions. Passive/malleable... is poison to the audience"; #6 "What is your character good at, comfortable with? Throw the polar opposite at them"; #15 honesty of feeling "lends credibility to unbelievable situations" — [Laughing Squid](https://laughingsquid.com/22-rules-of-storytelling-by-pixar-storyboard-artist/)
- Duolingo: Duo is "sassy and fiercely loyal", "that pushy friend that motivates you" (Zaria Parvez); the brand leaned into a "slightly menacing" image. Running gags: Duo vs. Dua Lipa confusion (incl. a "proposal" video, among the most watched), "do your lesson or else", parodies of Google Translate users (4.6M likes). TikTok launched Feb 2021; Duo became a regular character Oct 2021, after which viral content took off. Process: 1-2 h/day social listening, posts 3-5x/week only when there is an original idea, replies to comments in character — [Digiday, 13 Dec 2021](https://digiday.com/marketing/how-duolingo-is-using-its-unhinged-content-with-duo-the-owl-to-make-people-laugh-on-tiktok/)
- Mascot-centric Duolingo videos reportedly average 3-5M views (some 22M), and Duo-free content underperformed in comparison; "The comment section is our social brief" (Parvez) — [Adweek: Duo marketing strategy](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/duolingo-duo-owl-marketing-strategy/); TikTok's own case study frames Duo as a persistent persona — [TikTok Ads case study](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/ad-awards-duolingo-case-study)
- Novelty decay risk: Steak-umm's Nathan Allebach notes yearly viral spikes that wane, and questioned how long Duo's novelty would last — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/how-duolingo-is-using-its-unhinged-content-with-duo-the-owl-to-make-people-laugh-on-tiktok/)
- Serialized "event" arc: Duolingo announced Duo's death on 11 Feb 2025 (Cybertruck video, funerals, other mascots "killed off", a community "investigation"); on 17 Feb users were sent to a 50-billion-XP resurrection goal (XP earned by doing lessons); Duo returned ~a week later and on 25 Feb revealed it was staged ("I'm a mastermind") — [NPR, 26 Feb 2025](https://npr.org/2025/02/26/nx-s1-5309785/duolingo-owl-mascot-lives); [WECT](https://www.wect.com/2025/02/12/duo-duolingo-owl-has-died-company-says); a 1.7B impressions figure appears in a single secondary source only (unverified) — [Campaign: genius or confusing](https://campaignlive.com/article/duolingos-mascot-death-stunt-genius-confusing/1908041)
- BuzzFeed's Land of Boggs: aims for "really micro versions of a TV show"; core duo with contrasting temperaments (Boggo introvert / Boe extrovert), plus supporting cast (little brother, astrology-loving Tiffany); simple design rule ("colorful shape creatures with two limbs. That's the rule") makes production fast; BuzzFeed tests new characters yearly (Sanrio-style), keeping the ones that stick; ~5.5M TikTok followers, ~20M views/month; earlier content "used to be more genuine" with sad moments that weren't punchlines — [Tubefilter, May 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/05/11/creators-on-the-rise-the-land-of-boggs/amp/)
- Chikn Nuggit (Kyra Kupetsky, from 2020; on TikTok and YouTube) passed 1B combined views by June 2023 — [Wikipedia: Chikn Nuggit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikn_Nuggit)
- Invisible Universe (TikTok-native character studio) blends character development with commentary on current TikTok trends; stated ambition: "Pixar of the Internet" — [Shorty Awards entry](https://shortyawards.com/14th/tiktok-Invisible-Universe)

### Inferences
- Minuton's core "want vs. flaw": wants to be productive/creative, flaw = an irresistible thumb that drifts to the feed. Each episode = he tries (Pixar #1), fails comically, then converts the urge into a micro-creation (the app's promise) — the payoff is the brand promise without a hard sell (Duolingo: entertain, don't sell).
- Supporting cast should be contrast-based (Boggs model): e.g., a temptation character (the Feed/"Scrollo"), a calm creative friend, a notification gremlin. Keep designs as simple as Boggs' "two limbs" rule to sustain 2+ episodes/week.
- Default to episodic (each video stands alone, discoverable by cold viewers), with occasional serialized "events" (2-5 parts) tied to an in-app action, Duo-style.
- Catchphrase/sonic signature: Road Runner's "Beep-beep" and Duo's "do your lesson" show that one repeated line or sound becomes the character's identity; give Minuton one (e.g., a recurring sound when the thumb twitches).

### Gaps
- No academic study found on what makes brand mascots lovable in short-form specifically.
- No 2024-2026 in-depth interviews with short-form animators about character arcs were retrieved (Tubefilter 2022 is the most detailed).

## 4. Humor and emotion; wordless/visual storytelling vs. voice-over

### Takeaway
Humor theory (benign violation) maps neatly to the doomscroll theme: something "wrong" (being swallowed by the feed) made safe (cute mascot, playful framing). Wordless pantomime has a strong pedigree (Pixar shorts, Road Runner) and travels across languages and sound-off viewing, but some viewers find it lifeless without voice; mixing an emotional beat with gags broadens range.

### Cited Findings
- Benign Violation Theory: humor occurs when (1) something is a violation, (2) it is benign, and (3) both perceptions are simultaneous; violations become benign via weak commitment to the norm, psychological distance, or an alternative (playful) interpretation — [McGraw & Warren 2010, Psychological Science (PDF)](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/mcgraw.warren.2010.pdf); incongruity alone does not separate funny from non-funny — [Warren & McGraw 2016, JPSP](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jpspy/pdf/10.1037/pspi0000041~differentiating-what-is-humorous-from-what-is-not)
- Pixar shorts (Knick Knack, For the Birds, One Man Band, Presto) tell stories "with Buster Keaton-like skill", largely wordless — [NBC News on Presto](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna25378152); Pixar shorts rely on minimal dialogue, expressive character animation, body language and music — [UNC: visual storytelling](https://whenindome.web.unc.edu/2009/07/10/visual-story-telling/)
- Counterpoint: some viewers found the dialogue-free Pixar Popcorn shorts "like a pantomime which really saps the life from the characters", while others praised them — [IMDb reviews](https://www.imdb.de/title/tt13634444/reviews)
- Road Runner: "No dialogue ever, except 'Beep-beep!'" — [MeTV Toons](https://metvtoons.com/lists/chuck-jones-rules-of-the-road-runner)
- BuzzFeed tests jokes by asking "what's the funniest version of something?" and judges by shareability (would someone tag a friend / send it to a sibling?); emotional, non-punchline sad moments were part of earlier Boggs content — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/05/11/creators-on-the-rise-the-land-of-boggs/amp/)
- OpusClip: use one emotional register in the opening, with music, face, captions and cut aligned — [OpusClip 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-tiktoks-first-3-seconds-2026)

### Inferences
- Benign-violation recipe for Minuton: violation = the absurd horror of doomscrolling (thumb possessed, feed as a monster, time disappearing); benign = mascot cuteness, cartoon physics, the "it's just a minute" framing. Too real (shaming the viewer) breaks "benign"; too soft loses the "violation".
- Recommended default: wordless or near-wordless core action (pantomime + SFX + 1 line of on-screen text), with optional short VO for punchlines. This works sound-off, works for international audiences, and gives the character a signature non-verbal voice (gibberish/sounds, like Beep-beep).
- Alternate pure-gag episodes with occasional sincere/"tender" ones (Boggs) to deepen attachment.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found comparing wordless vs. VO-narrated short-form animation performance.

## 5. Editing rhythm: shot length, cuts, captions, VO pacing in French, sound design, music, silence

### Takeaway
Hollywood average shot length fell to ~2.5-4 s, and modern editing follows near-1/f rhythm patterns matching attention; short-form practice is faster but no rigorous public benchmark exists. French VO runs ~120-160 words/min (≈2-2.7 words/s), and subtitles should be readable at ~12-15 characters/second. Captions and sound both matter; sound-off share is disputed.

### Cited Findings
- Cutting et al. (2011), 160 English-language films 1935-2010: shot lengths got shorter, reflecting filmmakers' increasing control of viewers' attention — [i-Perception, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485803/); ASL fell from ~8-10 s in the 1960s to ~3-4 s by 2005 (2010 Psychological Science summary), Cutting cites ~12 s (1930) vs ~2.5 s today — [Flick Filosopher](https://www.flickfilosopher.com/2014/02/people-complain-fast-edits-modern-films-need-know.html); shot-length sequences increasingly follow 1/f patterns similar to human attention fluctuations — [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/271563)
- French speech rate: average 120-160 words/min — [Omni Calculator (FR)](https://www.omnicalculator.com/fr/vie-quotidienne/calculateur-mots-par-minute); tool presets slow 110 / normal 140 / fast 170 wpm — [Miniwebtool](https://miniwebtool.com/fr/calculateur-de-temps-de-parole/); ~130 wpm used for VO timing — [Sozai word counter](https://sozai.app/fr/tools/word-counter/) (calculators, not standards)
- Subtitles: Belgian CSA recommends 12-15 characters/second reading speed, 2 lines (3 max) — [CSA presentation](https://bdf.belgium.be/media/static/files/import/ca_bdf_2019_06_18/07b-csa-2019-06-05-presentation-gs-qualite.pdf)
- Captions: secondary report of TikTok research that accessibility-style captions increase watch time, completion and recall — [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/the-tiktok-creative-codebook-sound-on-native-first-90-10-rule); caption the first line within 0.5 s, large, high contrast — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-tiktoks-first-3-seconds-2026)
- Sound-on vs off conflict: "88% of TikTok viewing is sound-on" (attributed to TikTok, no date) — [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/the-tiktok-creative-codebook-sound-on-native-first-90-10-rule); vs. "~40% of first views auto-play sound-off in 2026" (no source) — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-tiktoks-first-3-seconds-2026)
- Cut ~0.3 s before a beat's natural end; motion within 1 s; audio within 0.5 s, voice peaks -6 to -3 dB, 200 ms fade-in on music — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-tiktoks-first-3-seconds-2026)
- Duolingo content riffs on trending audio — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/how-duolingo-is-using-its-unhinged-content-with-duo-the-owl-to-make-people-laugh-on-tiktok/)

### Inferences
- VO word budgets in French (derived from 120-160 wpm ≈ 2-2.7 words/s; keep ~20-30% silence for gags and SFX): 15 s ≈ 20-30 words max; 30 s ≈ 45-60 words; 60 s ≈ 90-120 words. For punchy comedic VO, aim at the lower bound and let visuals carry.
- On-screen text at 12-15 chars/s: a 3-s text card holds ~36-45 characters (~6-8 French words). Hook captions should fit that.
- Shot rhythm (inferred, not benchmarked): new visual information every ~1-2 s in setup/escalation, then a deliberate hold (0.5-1.5 s of silence/stillness) right before the punchline — the "beat" that makes the gag land; vary shot lengths rather than metronomic cuts (Cutting's 1/f finding).
- Sound design: a signature SFX for Minuton (thumb twitch, "scroll" whoosh), a short musical sting for the payoff, and a silence before the twist. Avoid an audible "outro" if looping.

### Gaps
- No credible public benchmark on cuts-per-second or average shot length specific to TikTok/Shorts/Reels animation.
- No official French standard on VO pacing for social video; numbers come from calculators. No reliable study on caption effect size.

## 6. Writing templates / beat sheets used by short-form storytellers

### Takeaway
Usable templates are mostly practitioner-derived: Story Spine compressed, Hook-Promise-Payoff (open loop), gag beat (setup-escalation-pause-punch-button), and series "rules bibles" (Road Runner). Production models borrowed from TV ("micro versions of a TV show") and rapid testing are standard at studios like BuzzFeed.

### Cited Findings
- Story Spine template (6-line Pixar version; 8-line Adams version incl. "Ever since then") — [Laughing Squid](https://laughingsquid.com/22-rules-of-storytelling-by-pixar-storyboard-artist/); [NPR 2026](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5750619)
- Pixar #7 (ending first) and #22 (most economical telling) as writing process — [Laughing Squid](https://laughingsquid.com/22-rules-of-storytelling-by-pixar-storyboard-artist/)
- Rules bible approach (Chuck Jones) — [MeTV Toons](https://metvtoons.com/lists/chuck-jones-rules-of-the-road-runner)
- BuzzFeed animation: TV-studio production model at micro scale, 2 videos/week per series, one person can finish a video in ~a week once script and voice are done; Shorts as "a great testing ground"; results differ by platform (a Zodiac video won on TikTok, flopped on YouTube); compilations of shorts repackaged for long YouTube — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/05/11/creators-on-the-rise-the-land-of-boggs/amp/)
- Duolingo: daily social listening, comments as brief, only post with an original idea — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/how-duolingo-is-using-its-unhinged-content-with-duo-the-owl-to-make-people-laugh-on-tiktok/); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/duolingo-duo-owl-marketing-strategy/)
- Each series video should deliver one clear payoff — [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/youtube-shorts-strategy-a-30-day-plan-for-creators); diagnose drop-off points on each Short's retention graph — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-low-watch-time-boost); 70-85% retention cited as a strong target for <60 s Shorts (attributed to prepublish.ai, unverified) — [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/de/blog/best-retention-strategies-youtube-shorts)

### Inferences
- Beat sheet A, "Gag court" 15-20 s: 0-1.5 s hook image (Minuton + problem) / 1.5-8 s attempt + escalation (2-3 beats) / 8-9 s pause / 9-13 s punchline (urge → micro-creation) / 13-15 s button that loops to frame 1.
- Beat sheet B, "Mini-histoire" 30-45 s (Story Spine): hook in media res (0-2 s) / normal (2-5 s) / because of that x2-3 (5-25 s) / until finally twist (25-35 s) / ever since then = loop or running-gag tag (35-40 s).
- Beat sheet C, "Épisode de série" 45-60 s: "Previously" 1-line caption (0-2 s) / mini-goal / obstacle / mini-payoff delivered / new complication as cliffhanger in last 3 s, labelled "Partie 2".
